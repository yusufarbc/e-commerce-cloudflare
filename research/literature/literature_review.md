# Serverless Edge Platforms and Security Gates: Literature Scan (v3, after citation chaining)

In the sources retrieved, no study evaluates CI/CD security gates on an isolate-based edge platform, none computes an order-independent attribution of detection across three or more heterogeneous control classes, and none compares detection by input source (an HTTP request versus a queue, cron, storage or webhook event). That statement is limited to what this search could see (last section) and belongs in the paper as "we did not find", not "there is none".

Two qualifications are new in this version. First, the comparison and combination of static analysis tools is an established topic that reaches back to at least 2009. The earlier version of this scan under-reported it because its first sweep was restricted to 2022 onward; the citation-chaining pass recovered the older studies. Whether any of them attributes detection to individual tools is not established, because the two most likely candidates (Austin et al. 2012 and Nunes et al. 2018) are closed access and I did not read them. Second, the mechanism that the twin-corpus design tests, namely that event-chain semantics defeat static analysis, is already asserted as a motivation by a 2026 serverless analysis tool. That is a claim about research prototypes, and I found no measurement of it on default CI gates.

## Platform background

### Two meanings of edge

"Edge computing" names two largely separate literatures, and the one that dominates search results is not the one this paper concerns. Of the 269 candidate records the edge strand produced, 118 (44%) mention at least two terms from a list covering mobile or multi-access edge, 5G, IoT, vehicular networks, offloading and federated learning in their title or abstract; in that literature the edge is a telecom or device tier and the research questions are offloading, scheduling and latency. The paper's edge is a software platform: functions executed inside lightweight isolates at a content-delivery network's points of presence.

The clearest published statement of that second meaning is a 2026 architecture and performance study of seven platforms, which reports that a second generation of serverless platform has emerged, that first-generation platforms rely on containerised, centralised execution while the new generation uses lightweight isolates and edge deployment, and that the change cuts warm-request latency from roughly 40 ms to roughly 10 ms while limiting the execution environment ([Schirmer et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3841482)). The same vocabulary split appears within serverless-edge papers themselves: a 2020 study of latency-sensitive applications on "public serverless edge cloud platforms" concerns AWS and its Greengrass IoT edge extension, and its abstract does not mention isolate runtimes ([Pelle et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1109/jiot.2020.3042428)). A reader who searches "serverless edge" will land in either literature, so the paper should define the term in its first paragraph and cite the 2026 study for it.


### Serverless architecture background

The general serverless literature is large, mature and mostly concerned with performance and programming model. A multivocal review of FaaS performance evaluation is a systematic entry point for the performance strand ([Scheuner and Leitner 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jss.2020.110708)). For framing, the widely cited overviews are available as [Jonas et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.4230/oasics.microservices.2020-2022.5), [Castro et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1145/3368454) and the survey by [Shafiei et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1145/3510611). I confirmed that these exist and recorded their metadata, but the retrieval returned no abstracts for them, so this document says nothing about their content beyond their titles.

Two developer-facing empirical studies bear on the paper's motivation: [Leitner et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jss.2018.12.013) on FaaS development in industrial practice and [Wen et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1145/3468264.3468558) on application-development challenges. Whether either reports on non-HTTP triggers is not established; the retrieved records had no abstract, and that claim must be checked in the full text before it supports the event-source hypothesis.


### Platform-side and application-side threats

Security research on edge platforms concentrates on the provider's isolation boundary, not on defects in tenant code. Cloudflare Workers removes process isolation between tenants in favour of language-level isolation, which exposes co-located tenants to Spectre-class attacks; [Schwarzl et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2110.04751) demonstrated a remote attack on that model and proposed Dynamic Process Isolation, and [Schwarzl et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2608.17043) showed that the production implementation of that defence was insufficient, leaking a JWT from a co-located worker before Cloudflare deployed further mitigations. The sandboxing layer beneath such platforms is itself an active research area: [Kolosick et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1145/3498688) note that Fastly and Cloudflare use software-based fault isolation to co-locate untrusted tenants, and [Cabrera-Arteaga et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1145/3560828.3564007) diversify WebAssembly services deployed on Fastly's edge so that one payload cannot compromise every node. Serverless-specific abuse of the billing model forms a third class ([Kelly et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jisa.2021.102843)).

These are the provider's responsibility and are invisible to source-level scanners. That is a scope boundary rather than a gap, and the threat-model section should state it: the gates measured here can only address defects in the tenant's own code and configuration. This distinction is my reading of the literature, not something any of these papers argues.


## Evidence on tools, benchmarks and methods

### Static analysis evaluation, combination and practice

**Practice and configuration.** A 2024 survey of 1,263 developers found that only 20% (204 of 1,003 screened) used SAST tools at all; among those who did, 59% did not use multiple tools, 54% did not configure them, and 40% did neither ([Bennett et al. 2024, survey](https://doi.org/10.1145/3674805.3690750)). This supports the premise that a default-configuration gate is the realistic baseline, and it is the direct motivation for measuring default rules before custom ones.

**Detection rates and combination.** A study of four SAST tools on production code with known vulnerabilities found individual detection rates of 11.2% to 26.5%, 38.8% for the four combined, and 44.7% for a newly configured Semgrep ([Bennett et al. 2024, Semgrep*](https://doi.org/10.1145/3661167.3661262)). The authors attribute part of the misses to missing vulnerable code patterns in the tools' rules, which is the closest published precedent for the custom-rule experiment. It is one of several combination studies, not the only one: the chaining pass retrieved, at title level only, an empirical study on combining diverse static analysis tools for web security vulnerabilities ([Nunes et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00607-018-0664-z)), a benchmark of static analysis tools for web security by an overlapping author group ([Nunes et al. 2018, IEEE TR](https://doi.org/10.1109/tr.2018.2839339)), an exploratory study on using machine learning to combine alerts from several SAST tools ([Pereira et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1109/ladc48089.2019.8995685)), a comparison of six tools that reports detection, agreement and precision ([Lenarduzzi et al. 2022, SSRN preprint](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4044439)), and a study that ran 24 tools on open-source repositories ([Hashmat et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-80020-7_13)). Their abstracts were not retrieved, so the sentence above describes their titles only.

**Comparing technique classes.** Comparisons across classes of technique exist for web services and web applications: penetration testing against static code analysis for SQL injection ([Antunes and Vieira 2009](https://doi.org/10.1109/prdc.2009.54)), a benchmarking approach for vulnerability detection tools ([Antunes and Vieira 2014](https://doi.org/10.1109/tsc.2014.2310221)), and a comparison of the efficiency and effectiveness of vulnerability discovery techniques ([Austin et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.infsof.2012.11.007)). Separately, an arXiv paper on SAST, DAST and penetration testing for Java applications ([arXiv 2208.01595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.01595)) is the only earlier study my first scan coded as measuring marginal contribution. I have read none of these full texts. If one of them reports the marginal or order-independent contribution of each technique, it removes part of the methodological novelty, so reading Austin et al. and the Antunes and Vieira papers is the most valuable remaining literature task.

**Secondary studies.** Two systematic reviews of SAST tool evaluation appeared in 2025, one covering SonarQube, Checkmarx and Bandit in CI/CD ([Dalaq et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3727967.3756838)) and one, in Portuguese, a systematic mapping of SAST tool comparisons ([Sousa et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.5753/sbseg_estendido.2025.11875)); mining their inclusion lists is a cheaper way to find precedents than further keyword searches. An interview study with twenty industry professionals asks why they use SAST benchmarks and what barriers they see ([Li et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3729404)); its abstract cites recent research suggesting that existing benchmarks often fail industry's needs, which bears on how the corpus should be justified.

**Multi-gate pipelines and overhead.** The closest multi-gate pipeline study found is a 2025 master's thesis that operationalises OWASP ASVS Level 1 in CI/CD by orchestrating seven open-source scanners across SCA, SAST, DAST and secret detection, evaluated on one open-source project and one industrial case study (OpenAlex W7133037724, Tampere University repository, no DOI). It reports per-tool runtimes (Dependency-Check 78 s, Semgrep 111 s, Trivy 47 s, and ZAP 56 s on Flowise; the abstract does not state which target the first three refer to) and a 73.6% reduction in non-actionable Semgrep alerts from generic rules. Its abstract describes no injected ground-truth corpus, no per-control attribution and no edge target. A 2026 evaluation of a DevSecOps pipeline for multi-tier PHP applications on AWS with Jenkins proposes a proxy design that decouples intensive scanning from the primary build flow to avoid bottlenecks ([Rida et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/fi18070371)); it supports reporting critical-path time separately from total scanner time, though I read only the first part of its abstract.

### Serverless static analysis and event chains

FaaSGuard is the closest pipeline paper: a secure CI/CD design for OpenFaaS evaluated on 20 real-world serverless functions (arXiv 2509.04328). CloudFlow (USENIX Security 2025) identifies security-sensitive data flows in serverless applications on Python with the Pysa engine, and Valve secures function workflows ([Datta et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1145/3366423.3380173)). A 2019 HotCloud paper constructs static call graphs for AWS Lambda applications (Obetz et al.; no DOI or abstract retrieved). The 2026 SymFlow paper states that static tools such as CloudFlow struggle with dynamic event chains, because trigger-handler relationships depend on conditional logic, asynchronous callbacks and resource state, and that this yields both false positives and false negatives ([Wang et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3814943.3816168)). That is the same mechanism the twin corpus isolates, stated by the authors of a competing analysis tool rather than measured on default gates. An empirical study of 88 security issues across ten open-source serverless platforms found certificate and encryption, permission and role management, and authentication among the most frequent categories, with misconfigurations among the primary causes ([Hamza et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3727967.3756839)); this supports including configuration gates, though the platforms are not isolate-based.

### Benchmark validity and injected defects

The design injects defects into a purpose-built corpus, so evidence on synthetic-defect validity is a threat-to-validity issue, not background. A study of synthetic bugs found that fuzzers detect them significantly more easily than organic bugs, and that no fuzzer found any of 50 organic CVE bugs ([Bundt et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1145/3433210.3453096)); the study concerns fuzzing, not static analysis, so it motivates a separate real-defect arm and a stated limitation without predicting the SAST outcome. Related retrieved work, read at title level, covers benchmark construction and its critique: benchmarks built from positive and negative (secure) test cases, examined in [Miltenberger et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3579856.3582830); a 2015 workshop paper on test suites of intentionally implemented defects with evaluation criteria ([Shiraishi et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1109/issrew.2015.7392027)); a differential benchmark for SAST tools ([Delta-Bench, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1109/esem.2017.24)); the similarity of artificial and real faults in mutation-testing studies ([Ojdanic et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1109/tse.2023.3277564)); and 2026 work on vulnerability-seeded code synthesis for SAST benchmarks ([Khan 2026](https://doi.org/10.1109/iccsdfai70505.2026.11647897)) and an automated DAST benchmark framework ([Deeb et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1109/isdfs69419.2026.11459007)). The safe-twin (negative case) design is consistent with this benchmark tradition.

### Statistics, small samples and timing variability

Venue-restricted searches found what keyword searches missed. On analysis with few units: a review of robust statistical methods for empirical software engineering notes that non-robust methods can fail to detect important results ([Kitchenham et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10664-016-9437-5)); a 2024 paper gives recommendations for analysing small-sample software engineering experiments, where the data can be non-normal ([Kitchenham and Madeyski 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10664-024-10504-1)); and a systematic review of statistical power in software engineering experiments exists ([Dybå et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.infsof.2005.08.009); no abstract retrieved). My reading is that these support exact paired tests, effect sizes with confidence intervals and an explicit power statement for a corpus of 15 to 30 twin pairs, and argue against reporting p-values alone.

On time cost, the relevant precedents concern variability in cloud-hosted measurement: microbenchmarking in the cloud ([Laaber et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10664-019-09681-1); no abstract retrieved), performance-test stability for serverless applications ([Eismann et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jss.2022.111294)) and serverless performance variance ([Wen et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10664-025-10615-3)). None concerns CI runner time for security scanners, so they justify repeated runs with dispersion reporting but do not supply a protocol.

For Shapley attribution, the SE-venue searches returned no study that uses Shapley values to attribute detection to tools: the five venue searches returned 40 matches in total (14, 1, 5, 7 and 13 in EMSE, TSE, IST, JSS and TOSEM), and only two of them mention Shapley in the title or abstract, both concerning explanations of machine-learning models (abstracts were absent for some records). The theoretical source is [Shapley 1953](https://doi.org/10.1515/9781400881970-018). This is an absence in a bounded search, not a finding that none exists in security or ensemble-learning venues, which I did not search this way.

### API testing and the ecosystem

For the DAST arm and the authorization cases, surveys of REST API testing (92 articles, [Golmohammadi et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3617175)) and a comparison of seven fuzzers on open-source, industrial and artificial APIs ([Zhang and Arcuri 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3597205)) describe the state of tools, and a 2025 paper detects broken object-level authorization from OpenAPI specifications via coloured Petri nets ([Santos Filho et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10207-024-00970-5)). I expect an unauthenticated ZAP baseline to miss object-level authorization defects; the corpus measures that, it is not established here.

For the platform's own tooling, OpenAlex returned 3 records for Hono, 4 for D1, 7 for Prisma with raw queries and none for wrangler configuration, all theses or unrelated. I found no peer-reviewed empirical work on these components, so the paper should cite official documentation (a grey-literature source with version and access date) for their behaviour. One 2026 record on build-aware scanning of JavaScript applications ([Sarva 2026](https://doi.org/10.52783/jisem.v11i3s.15006)) is unread.

## What the search can and cannot support

The search log has 139 rows (105 OpenAlex, 33 arXiv, one snowball; nine pilot rows have no retained counts; details in `search_protocol.md` and `search_log.csv`). The first two sweeps merged 4,276 unique records and reduced them lexically to 789 candidates. A model coded 459; 330 were not coded because the model-call token ceiling was reached, and the shortfall fell hardest on the newest topics (65% of the candidates reached only through edge or serverless axes). I read 103 of the 789 candidates, and I did not read the other 320 or so uncoded titles individually. The citation-chaining pass then used 67 seed works (42 returned at least one neighbour, 25 returned none; cause not checked) and retrieved 2,268 neighbour records, of which 1,707 were new; of the 174 linked to two or more seeds, 27 key records were retrieved with full metadata (10 had abstracts), and the roughly 1,800 snowball-only records are metadata only. Venue-restricted method searches (25 queries) are logged separately.

The model's labels are not reliable enough to count from. Checking its 34 "core" records against their abstracts found a platform attack labelled as a scanner evaluation, a Node.js study labelled serverless, CI-platform labels on SAST papers unrelated to CI, two "marginal attribution" flags that I judged are not (one paper coded differently across two records), and a multi-gate thesis labelled as having no timing data. Counts from the earlier positioning table (36 core studies; one with marginal attribution; none on edge) are therefore approximate, and the classic-SAST layer added here was absent from them. The claims that matter most, no edge-platform gate evaluation and no attribution across heterogeneous control classes, rest on reading the candidates, not on the flags.

Remaining gaps, in order of value:

1. Read Austin et al. 2012, Nunes et al. 2018 and the Antunes and Vieira papers (closed access here; needs institutional access or PDFs from the user), then decide whether any reports per-technique marginal contribution.
2. Mine the inclusion lists of the two 2025 SAST reviews.
3. Model-code the roughly 330 uncoded candidates and screen the roughly 3,487 lexically dropped records, which requires model calls that were unavailable in this session.
4. Query IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library and Scopus directly, and search TR Dizin and DergiPark, none of which this environment can reach.
5. Retrieve grey literature (NIST SSDF revision status, OWASP CI/CD Top 10, OWASP Serverless Top 10, SLSA, Cloudflare documentation); the SLSA and OWASP entries in `refs.bib` were written from memory.
6. Retrieve abstracts for the 13 of 22 reading-list entries that lack them, and check the CloudFlow page range.
7. Rerun the logged queries immediately before submission and record the date, since OpenAlex counts change.
